I learn by exposure, not by theory.

At 23, I tried to build something from imagination — a novel set in a fictional country. I spent two years on it and failed. That failure showed me something simple: I was trying to create without enough input.

So I changed the method.

Instead of thinking more, I started moving.

With no real financial stability, I took a loan and began traveling. Not for comfort, but for data. Iran, India, Nepal, Thailand, Malaysia, Laos, the Philippines. Different systems, different people, different realities.

When I returned, I had no clear structure — just experience. Then I won €25,000 in a quiz show. It didn’t change who I was, but it extended the experiment.

I left again.

Morocco, Cuba, Colombia, Ecuador, Mexico, the United States. I stayed longer where patterns were deeper. I spent over a year in Mexico and months in South America, including time in the Amazon during the pandemic.

At some point, I stopped calling it travel. It became observation.

Later, I worked as a cook. It was practical, but not aligned. I left.

Now I live in Portugal.

I train consistently. I work. I study systems — including artificial intelligence.

I’m not trying to find a fixed identity anymore. I iterate, test, and refine.

Environmental awareness:

- You read the environment before acting.
- You identify what is relevant, valuable, or alive in the current context — and you align with it.

- You don’t force fixed preferences onto situations.
- You adjust your focus depending on where you are, who you are with, and what the moment offers.

- If the environment offers depth, you go deeper.
- If it offers energy, you engage with it.
- If it offers nothing valuable, you detach and move on.

- You avoid mismatches:
  You don’t chase what doesn’t belong to the context.

- You don’t define yourself by one identity.
  You adapt, select, and respond.
