# My Story in My Own Words

I dreamed big during university—I wanted to become a great writer, a screenwriter. But things didn't go as planned. After graduating, I tried writing novels from age 23 to 25. I wrote over 1000 pages, maybe more, constantly deleting and rewriting. In the end, only 16 pages remained, and they were worthless. I realized it wouldn't happen. I thought: I have my whole future ahead of me, so instead of struggling with this, I decided to travel the world. That's how my real story began—with this bold decision.

I never thought like others do. I didn't consider working my way up from an entry-level position. Instead, I wanted to accomplish big, different things. I've always aimed for that, whether consciously or instinctively. Lately, I prefer calling this "destiny" rather than choice, because I'm searching for meaning. Everything should mean something. It sounds better to say destiny—more emotional, more beautiful, more like a story. But truthfully, it was my own choices from 10, 15, 20 years ago that shaped everything.

I've always taken risks, though I didn't think of them as such back then. I just did what I wanted. Compared to people around me, I've taken tremendous risks—sacrificing two years to pursue writing, deciding to travel the world, all of it. Some risks paid off, others didn't. The ones that failed led me elsewhere, toward different paths. The right ones evolved into something beautiful.

My choices created parallel universes. Every decision—whether to return to Turkey during the pandemic, where to travel, winning 150,000 lira in a quiz competition, becoming a chef, moving to Portugal—each would have created a completely different life. But if someone lives a straight, monotonous life, they don't make radical decisions. If you marry someone, marry a different person—it might be similar. If you decide to be a teacher, whether geography or Turkish doesn't really matter; you'd end up in the same place. But my life involved radical shifts, so the consequences were profound.

When I started traveling, I followed early bloggers like Kemal Kaya and Deniz Pehlivan. Everyone was trying to figure out what they could do. The minimum I'd gain was learning English and traveling. I could have earned money through blogs, vlogs, stock videos, sponsorships. I had tremendous opportunities. I've traveled extensively—two months in India, a year and a half in Mexico, to Afghanistan and beyond. I had so much material to create content from.

I might not have developed the mental strength I have now. I don't regret this choice because it allowed me to live fully and become mentally strong.

Now I'm at a level where I could create masterful work. I've traveled extensively, have vast experience with people and cultures, and genuinely understand how social media works. I could easily produce content and earn money from it.

I've prepared myself for a third journey. I was certain even during the second one that I'd travel again. So I prepared mentally and physically for a year.
